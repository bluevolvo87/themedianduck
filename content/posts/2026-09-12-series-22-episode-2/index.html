Episode Recap

Episode 2 was a low scoring episode, with the winner only obtaining 14 points.

  • The first tiebreaker of the series took place in this episode; the tiebreaker was between between Matt, Nina and Richard, who were required to estimate the temperature of water in a Thermos flask.
    • It’s not entirely clear why the Thermos flask was greased; was for it maintaing a stable temperature and/or to make sure the task was fair for all contestants?
  • If I remember correctly, recent series were relatively sparse on episode tiebreakers, so its appearance in this episode was a welcome return.
    • We don’t however talk about the championship tiebreaker of Series 20 however as it is still painful that Ania was done dirty.1
  • This was a disqualification heavy episode; all contestants were awarded 0 points in at least one task attempt.
    • There have not been any point deductions however (yet).
  • We had our first task outing at this series’ special location, the London Museum of Water and Steam.
    • The task “Measure the tower in terms of baked beans.”, required contestants to measure, be comfortable with manipulating ratios, and being observant. Piece of cake, or should that be beans!
    • Measuring in the unit of baked beans is by no means a first in the Taskmaster UK universe.
      • In Series 7, we saw the contestants measure the perimeter of the caravan in terms of beans. From this attempt, there was a slightly arrogant Rhod Gilbert who uttered the term “mean bean”. He also got his imperial and metric measurements mixed up which must have been humbling.
      • Series 1’s Josh Widdicombe also provided an estimate of the number of beans in a can. This research finding is even brought up in the task “Set a number, and do the thing that number of times exactly.” which features later in the episode; Little Alex Hermes states there are about 400 to 500 baked beans in a can according to Dr. Widdicombe’s research. Well done to our trusted assistant for providing a range, rather than a single number, since there is likely going to be variability between cans.

The Mean Bean

Some specific remarks regarding the “Measure the tower in terms of beans.” terms.

  • A phrase that was often stated to us when preparing for exams at school and university was “Read the F*cking Question” . In a similar vein for the Taskmaster universe, “Read the F*cking Task” is appropriate with “Re-read it again. And again.” also being a suitable appendage for the phrase.
    • This advice would have served Nina extremely well who gave her measurement in terms of cans, instead of beans. This is despite being prompted by Little Alex Hermes multiple times, and a remark made during her entrance to this task that she was “going to try and be more observant”. Oh the irony!
  • It is somewhat surprising that none of these fully grown adults seemed capable of working and manipulating ratios to estimate the tower’s height in beans.
    • Maybe it’s because I am a (relatively) logical and numerical person, but this task doesn’t seem too convoluted. I would hope Dara Ó Briain (Series 14, physicist by training) , Phil Wang (Series 7, engineer by training) or Victoria Core Mitchell (Series 12, two time poker champion) would fare better, but pressure can do all sorts of things to even the most competent individual.
  • “When you assume, you make an ass out of you and me.” I’m glad Chloe clarified whether the bean measurements assumed whether the beans were standing vertically or horizontally. It can certainly help to clarify these assumptions to have a decent chance at providing an accurate estimate and potentially winning the task.
    • It also helps in this task to measure the correct tower that is being referred to, measuring in the correct units (beans rather than cans), and competently manipulating ratios.

Gamble’s Gamble

End-of-Series Ranking Distributions

Figure 10: Series Ranking Distribution: Using Data up to Episode 1 (left), and up to Episode 2 (right).

The Marginal distributions for each contestant (see Figure 10) have noticeably flatted; this is a reflection of the uncertainty of how this series will pan out. As indicated in the series tracker (Figure 8) , there is only 6 points separating the cast, and thus still anyone’s game.

In addition to the flattened distributions for series ranking, the many distributions have changed the location of their peak and center; the peaks are aligned with the current series standings as seen in Figure 8.

The most probable series ranking placement for each contestant are:

  • Chloe: 3rd with 45.79% probability; previously 1st with 92.18%.
  • Isy: 5th with 50.38% chance; previously 5th with 71.85%.
  • Matt: 1st with 46.38% probability; previously 3rd with 75.01%.
  • Nina: 1st with 50.38% chance; previously 2nd with 70.17%.
  • Richard: 4th with 35.30% probability; previously 4th with 73.97%.

Only Isy and Richard have retained the same most probable series ranking as inferred after Episode 1, but now with less probability and certainty assigned to these positions.

Joint Cast Ranking Distributions

Figure 11: Joint Distribution of Cast Series Ranking: Using Data up to Episode 1 (left), and up to Episode 2 (right).

The joint distribution for how the entire cast will place (see Figure 11) has also flattened significantly compared to Episode 1; again this is a reflection of the uncertainty surrounding the current series standings and how it will pan out in subsequent episodes..

The most probable cast ranking is [1st: Matt, 2nd: Nina, 3rd: Chloe, 4th: Richard, 5th: Isy] with 10.24% chance of occurring.

However, there are 3 other cast ranking combinations which are near equally likely. These vary on the following axis of:

  • Whether Matt or Nina will be crowned series champion or runner up.
  • Whether Isy or Richard will be the series loser or not.

The former most probable cast ranking after Series 1, [1st: Chloe, 2nd: Nina, 3rd: Matt, 4th: Richard, 5th: Isy], only has an estimated 0.93% probability of occurring.

The 5th and 6th most probable outcomes with approximately 5% chance occurring has Chloe ending in 4th place. She likely won’t be happy with this outcome if it becomes a reality.

What Have We Learnt Today?

Figure 13: What really is the point of talking, or anything else for that matter?

We’ve learnt that:

  • Despite his relatively “laissez-faire” attitude (maybe this should be low effort and energy) in many tasks thus far, Richard secured his first episode win of the series.
  • This episode was a low scoring episode for all with all cast members receiving at least one instance of 0 points for a task attempt.
    • Richard managed to win the episode with just 14 points, and this was also the first appearance of a tiebreaker in the series. The tiebreaker was between Richard, Matt and Nina.
  • Despite Richard’s episode win, it is Nina and Matt who are currently leading the series, but this is a close series at the moment with 6 points separating the entire cast.
    • It would not surprise me if the series standings change again after Episode 3.
  • This uncertainty of how the series will pan out is reflected in the generated distributions for how contestants will place (each individually and collectively), as they are incredibly flat.
    • The most probable cast ranking is [1st: Matt, 2nd: Nina, 3rd: Chloe, 4th: Richard, 5th: Isy] with 10.24% chance of occurring.
    • This flatness and uncertainty should not be seen as a flaw, more that the current series standings are incredibly tight, and not indicative for the rest of the series.
  • Captain Cuddles is surprised that many of the grown adults of this cast are unable to manipulate ratios to measure a tower in beans accurately.
  • Captain Cuddles still hasn’t gotten over the Series 20’s championship tiebreaker controversy and that Ania lost. Ania won in other ways however, namely securing a place as a cast member of SNL UK.
