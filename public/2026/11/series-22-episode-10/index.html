My Subjective Review

It is my strong belief that there are no inherently bad series of Taskmaster UK (so far), but some definitely don’t hit the mark as much as others.

Unfortunately, Series 22 didn’t quite hit the mark for me compared to my favourite series (4, 5 and 19 are my favourite ones).

In general, I felt that the chemistry and banter between the cast was lacking compared to prior series. There were certainly some moments of brilliance (Isy’s prize task and reasoning for example), but nothing as memorable as J.V. Martzoukas, “Friendship is Truth”, “We share the same juice…”, “He’s so famous!” or _“Rosalind’s a f*cking nightmare”_.

I do think that at least two contestants of the Series 22 cast were not as familiar with the show and were less committed with some of their task attempts. I’m mainly looking at Matt and Richard. Whilst previous series have had at least one contestant who was less familiar with the show and not trying their hardest, they were usually able to revert this in the studio and argue their way through to much entertainment (see Judi Love in Series 13), and the remaining contestants were able to rectify this.

On a slight sidenote, I rewatched the whole Junior Taskmaster series last weekend and even that series was more entertaining that Series 22; I would happily watch a full series of some of those minor contestants!

The Overestimation of Richard Ayoade

Part of this series disappointment may be rooted in the high hopes we had for Richard Ayoade as a contestant; he is someone that has been on the contestant wishlist amongst the fandom.

We likely expected Richard to be a contestant who would challenge the entire premise of the show and refuse to do the tasks to his full ability (mostly for comedic effect, see his numerous appearances and performances on Big Fat Quiz of the Year). To this point, Richard delivered on this expectation with near perfect precision. However, refusing to conform to the show for nearly all tasks and episodes eventually got tedious.

The main broadcasted edit of the show lacked the most of Richard’s rebuttals for his (poor) performance, which is likely where the main humour and entertainment lies when Richard appears on other panel shows. The outtakes do show that these rebuttals did occur and were entertaining. **Perhaps this is why the series was lacklustre from an Ayoade addition perspective?*8

Conti-nental Baggage

Nina’s frequent puppet related prize tasks got a bit tedious and frustrating after the 3rd episode or so. This definitely added to my disappointment in the series.

Nina definitely did seem to adopt the Daisy May Cooper, Sanjeev Bhaskar method of using existing possessions in their house as prize task submissions. However for Nina and her puppet heavy life, and under the Cooper-Bhaskar prize task method, puppets were many of the submissions and consequently there was little variation and consequential entertainment.

What is frustrating as a Conti-stan is that we know that Nina is a highly creative individual and she could have brought in and even created great prizes if she spent a bit more time and effort on the submissions. Maybe she was busy with more important things at this time?

From the pre-recorded tasks, Nina has stated that she is less of a planner and this may also potentially explain her weak effort in the prize tasks.

Upon reflection, Nina was a bit more meek and passive in the studio which may have also contributed to my disappointment in this series; she rarely argued back with the Taskmaster, or showed some true passion and vocalised her opinion. Perhaps if Nina was a bit more vocal, she could have increased her points allocation and be crowned series champion…

🎶 Taskmaster, tasks to be done 🎶

The tasks can of course make or break a series.

Tasks have tended to beome more complex over the evolution of Taskmaster as highlighted by other fans (see JamieDoesTV for example). This series was no exception, with the Gregulator potentially being one of the most complex tasks in recent history. Such overly complex tasks do impede the entertainment of the tasks and the show in general; they can be harder to reproduce at home, and often require a lot of more concentration to fully understand what to do (particularly in the second screen society that we live in today).

My biggest gripe with Taskmaster these days continues to be the over use of chance related studio tasks and winner takes all tasks, see “Nipple-Napkin-Knapsack-Knappett” and “snag a snake” tasks as good example of both. These tasks significantly lower the amount of jeopardy in a series (particularly with strong contenders such as Chloe), and thus impede my enjoyment of them. It is not particularly entertaining when a task is won by no to little skill, and was largerly random.

If anyone from Taskmaster UK is reading this, I would really appreciate it if you stopped these tasks. I’m happy to provide my services to the UK production at a significantly discounted rate!

End on a positive…

  • Isy’s baffling mind and the introduction of the Cutlery Plait and Lentil Piano to a global audience.
  • Nina’s distinct lewks between the episodes (aging rocker in the pre-records, 60s fashion model, glamorous librarian, glamorous ladies who lunch, Grecian queen).
  • The wholesome team dynamic of Nina and Isy (like two equally zany sisters who different in their own way).
  • Chloe frequently getting annoyed and arguing with Richard, especially with a significant lead
  • Richard asking if he can take the props used in the studio tasks home.
  • Matt asking if how the Taskmaster team were accounting for those who colourblind for the studio tasks.
